## Chapter 1: Getting Theology Right
_**Summary**: The key question isn't "do I agree with the Church on x, y, or z", but "Is the Catholic Church what it claims to be?" This book aims to give enough to believe that the Catholic Church, with the papacy, was instituted by Christ, and that we should be part of it._
- "If you want to know whether or not you should be a Catholic, there's only one issue you need to solve: is the papacy true or not?" It's not the most *important* doctrine, but it is the most *distinctive*..."Quite simply, if the Catholic Church's claims about the papacy are true, then everyone should be a Catholic. If they're not true, then nobody should be a Catholic. (15)
- Wrong Theology looks like this: beginning with a set of our own theological conclusions and then judging the Church by how well it accords with our own views; or researching a question, and then finding a denomination that fits what your belief is on that question. But this approach is unbiblical (cf. [[Heb-13#v17]], [[Jer-03#v15]]). (18)
- The proper question is, *"What is the Church?"* (19)
- "The Church holds itself out to be directly founded and sustained by Jesus Christ, in some mysterious way an extension of his incarnation...If that claim is true, we can trust the Church even if it contradicts our personal reading of Scripture...If the Church is what it says it is, then your objections to its teachings are wrong, even if you don't yet know *why* they're wrong." (21)
- [[St. Anselm]]'s *"faith seeking understanding"* is not *"faith contingent upon understanding"*. Peter doesn't claim to understand the teaching on the Eucharist: he simply accepts it on the basis of Jesus' identity as the Holy one of God ([[Jn-06#v68]]-69). He'll come to understand more in time. But he doesn't wait to believe until he fully understands. (22-23)
- "At a certain point, you've been given enough to trust, and conditioning your belief on getting yet more free answers or yet more free bread is a form of faithlessness or disobedience." (23)
